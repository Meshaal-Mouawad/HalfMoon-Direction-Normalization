ARTICLE INFO                                        ABSTRACT
Keywords:                                           Normalizing a point to unit direction from a chosen origin discards its distance, which may carry
Direction normalization                             information needed to classify it. We study the resulting loss in two crescent-shaped half-moon
Bayes risk                                          populations and ask whether moving the origin can preserve the missing decisions. We compare
Half-moon classification                            Bayes risks, the minimum attainable classification errors from raw coordinates and from direction
Spatial sign                                        alone. For absolutely continuous binary populations in dimension at least two, directional risk at
Radial quantization                                 distant origins converges uniformly to orthogonal-projection risk. Geometric criteria and computable
Neural networks                                     lower certificates then establish positive classification loss for every origin in specified disjoint and
                                                    overlapping half-moon configurations. In one disjoint configuration, adding a near/far radius bit to
                                                    direction restores every class decision; an exact threshold interval determines the sharp tolerance to
                                                    bounded radius-measurement error. Controlled neural studies comprise 500 candidate fits and 250
                                                    selected evaluations. In the independent repair experiment, direction-only networks made 54,362 errors
                                                    in 500,000 observations (10.8724%), whereas networks with the extra bit made three (0.0006%). These
                                                    results distinguish preprocessing-induced error from learned error and establish a minimal radial repair
                                                    for the constructed geometry.
