2     Research context and relationship to prior work

2.1    Direction and the geometry of normalized features

The spatial-sign transformation centers an observation and retains its unit direction, an
operation studied in robust multivariate analysis [1]. Work on normalized feature spaces
also addresses how classification procedures interact with angular feature geometry [2].
Such methods establish meaningful uses of direction representations. They do not by
themselves determine whether a given label decision is invariant to radius about every
possible origin.
    Recent research in Pattern Recognition Letters clarifies why geometry and deci-
sion rules must be distinguished. Poblete et al. [3] hold generative posterior-mean
embeddings fixed and compare radial, Gaussian probabilistic, linear discriminative and
tree-based classifiers across eight datasets. Classifier rankings vary with the dataset;
their class-centered radial-compatibility descriptor addresses the fit between a decision-
rule assumption and representation geometry. This identifies a compatibility question
within an existing representation. Our question concerns an earlier step: whether a
transformation into direction-only features has erased distinctions even an optimal
decision rule would require.
    Ahmadian and Lindsten [4] investigate time-series out-of-distribution detection
with radial-kernel scoring and context quantization. Their separate ablations show
that a normalized representation performs differently from an unnormalized radial-
kernel configuration on the principal activity datasets. The observed difference makes
lost magnitude information a plausible task-specific explanation. Their outcome
is novelty-detection performance, whereas the present work measures binary Bayes
classification risk under known continuous distributions. The studies are complementary
in motivation rather than directly comparable numerical benchmarks.
