origin cannot eliminate the loss.
   We quantify the effect using Bayes risk, the minimum misclassification probability
achievable with an optimal decision rule when the class distributions are known. The
risk for original coordinates captures ambiguity intrinsic to the task. The risk for
direction-only inputs measures what remains achievable after preprocessing. Their
difference is the additional error imposed by the representation, regardless of model
architecture or optimizer. A trained network may incur further error because of finite
samples, approximation and optimization; that excess must be evaluated against its
own representation-specific Bayes floor.
   Three questions structure this study. First, when does direction normalization
remove class information, and can any origin, including one arbitrarily far away, avoid
that loss? Second, when direction alone is insufficient, how much radial information is
necessary for the class decision, and how robust is the resulting code to measurement
error? Third, do neural classifiers fitted under a frozen protocol approach the corre-
sponding representation-specific Bayes risks? Each question requires distinct evidence:
mathematical identities and geometric proofs, a constructive decision rule and sharp
interval, and independent held-out neural evaluations.
   We derive an exact ray-cancellation identity for the loss, prove a uniform limit
connecting distant normalization origins to linear projection risk, and construct positive
whole-plane lower certificates. For a specified disjoint configuration, one common
near/far radius bit selects between two angular separators and correctly labels all
points in both continuous class supports. We then compare raw, direction-only, radius-
preserving, and one-bit representations in controlled studies comprising 500 candidate
fits and 250 selected test evaluations. The combined contribution is a population-level
explanation of when preprocessing destroys a decision, why choosing a new origin
cannot necessarily repair it, and how a minimal additional measurement can do so in
an explicitly proved setting.
