2.2    Decision sufficiency and representation error

Silva and Narayanan [5] treat signal representation in a Bayes-decision framework and
distinguish representational approximation from finite-data estimation. Further work
on decision sufficiency and lossless transformations establishes when a compressed
representation retains the optimal decision for a specified loss [6–8]. The general
principle is that information about an observation need not be preserved in full: what
matters for classification is whether the optimal label can still be determined.
   Direction normalization tests that principle on an explicit family of equivalence
classes. All points on the same positive ray from an origin have the same unit direction.
Dubois et al. [9] identify unit direction as an invariant to positive scaling when the
predictive task satisfies the corresponding invariance condition. If the preferred class
changes along some of these rays, the direction map violates the decision-sufficiency
condition. The present analysis asks how that violation depends on a moving common
origin and supplies an exact probability identity for its cost.


2.3    Projection boundaries and restricted radial recovery

Studies of projected classification and spherical models characterize decisions under
selected projections or geometric distribution assumptions [10–12]. From increasingly
distant origins, rays through a bounded population become nearly parallel, linking radial
viewing geometry to orthogonal projections [13]. Separability results for planar wedges
and strips provide geometric precedents for reasoning about mutually obstructing
class regions [14]. These strands motivate the need to handle both finite origins and
unbounded sequences of origins before claiming a strictly positive minimum over the
entire plane.
   A representation can also retain a small task-specific code rather than reconstruct
the discarded variable. Bayes-aware quantization and learned threshold research address
decision-relevant compression [15, 16]. In the present study, the code is deliberately
constrained to one common threshold applied to radius and two fixed angular separators.
