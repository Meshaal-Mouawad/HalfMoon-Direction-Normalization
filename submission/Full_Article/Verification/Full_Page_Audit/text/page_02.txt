1     Introduction

A preprocessing transformation changes what a classifier can learn before training
begins. Many transformations intentionally suppress aspects of an observation, such as
measurement scale, that may be irrelevant to a particular task. When an attribute
treated as nuisance variation also carries information needed for classification, its
removal creates an error that no subsequently trained classifier can avoid. Distinguishing
this information-induced error from imperfect model fitting is central to evaluating
representations rather than merely comparing the performance of learning algorithms.
    Direction normalization provides a concrete instance. Given an observation and
a common reference origin, it retains the unit vector pointing from the origin to the
observation and discards the distance along that direction. Such a representation is
meaningful when orientation, rather than magnitude, should govern the task; related
spatial-sign and normalized-feature transformations have established statistical and
classification uses [1, 2]. Yet two observations on the same ray receive identical features.
If those observations require different decisions, a classifier seeing only direction has no
variable with which to separate them. Whether this collapse causes a positive population
error, rather than an isolated geometric coincidence, depends on the underlying class
distributions.
    The half-moon problem makes the distinction explicit. Its two labels correspond
to thick semicircular regions: the upper half of a ring and a lower half-ring trans-
lated horizontally and vertically. Their relative positions produce both separable
and overlapping configurations while keeping the geometry analytically tractable. At
vertical displacement h = 0.5, choose the common midpoint c = (0.5, 0.25). The
class-0 observation x0 = (1, 0.25) and class-1 observation x1 = (2, 0.25) belong to
their respective half-annuli, yet normalize to the same direction (1, 0). Their distances
from the midpoint are 0.5 and 1.5. This constructed example identifies the discarded
distinction; a single near/far radius bit at threshold 0.8 distinguishes it. A theorem is
