   The frozen paired experiments instantiate this sequence. Their empirical outcomes
closely track the respective Bayes-risk floors across origins and distinguish those floors
from training excess. The independent repair study tests learnability of a theoretically
sufficient input rather than using neural accuracy to claim the existence of an exact
decoder. Together with the geometric proofs, these results demonstrate a specific
mechanism of decision-information loss and a precisely bounded method of recovery.


8.5    Limitations

The explicit half-moon results concern two-dimensional, continuously distributed half-
annular supports with specified radii and center displacement. The general uniform
projection-limit theorem covers absolutely continuous binary populations in dimension
at least two, whereas the specific geometric corollaries and positive numerical certifi-
cates require connected preferred-decision regions, convex-hull relations and density
assumptions. The certificates are conservative lower bounds over all origins; fixed-origin
risks are separate calculated values. The additional radial-bit construction and sharp
threshold interval are proved for the stated disjoint midpoint geometry, exact direction,
fixed angular rules and bounded additive radius error.
   The neural tests use equal-prior area-uniform observations, ten seeds per final
study, a specified one-hidden-layer architecture, a fixed training budget and prespeci-
fied model-selection procedures. Their results concern the studied distributions and
models. The radius-uniform comparison addresses analytical population risk, not
an additional neural training arm. Fitted errors include finite-sample, optimization
and approximation effects; some three-input controls have more parameters than the
two-input networks. These restrictions delimit the empirical claims and do not alter
the established mathematical results for their stated domains.
