This establishes a precise question: can one bit, used together with direction, recover
the correct decision everywhere on the specified supports, and what perturbation of
the measured radius can be tolerated?
    Taken together, this literature establishes the value of directional representations,
the need for decision-aware sufficiency criteria, and mathematical tools for projection
and quantization. The specific gap addressed here is an origin-wide population-risk
account for nonlinear direction normalization, linked to a minimal and explicitly
certified radial decision repair.



3     Study geometry, representations and inferential

      design

3.1    Why three half-moon arrangements are necessary

The half-moon family changes the vertical displacement h of one band while keeping
its width and horizontal displacement fixed. The geometry is deliberately continuous:
conclusions about population decisions should not depend on the particular finite points
sampled for an experiment. The class supports, their probability densities, and the
loss incurred by angular projection are defined formally in the next sections.
    Three configurations serve different inferential purposes. The h = −0.25 arrange-
ment is a negative control in which suitable origins can preserve all class decisions
after direction normalization. At h = 0.5, the supports are disjoint, so full coor-
dinates classify them without Bayes error, yet their arrangement forces directional
ambiguity from every origin. At h = 1, the original regions overlap, creating intrinsic
raw-coordinate ambiguity before any normalization; direction adds another component
of error. Comparing the three makes it possible to distinguish a representation defect
from a genuinely ambiguous source distribution.
