5.3    Candidate selection, test isolation and diagnostic arms

The principal factorial design specifies 300 candidates. At h = 1, prespecified width-15
and unconditioned width-64 diagnostics add 60 candidate fits each for R, DA , PA . The
main final experiment therefore comprises 420 candidate fits and 210 selected models.
These final fits are distinct from a preceding 48-fit development stage using seeds 101
and 103. Protocols were fixed before generating and evaluating the final test sets, so
their reported errors are held-out outcomes rather than checkpoint-selection statistics.
   The one-bit repair experiment later adds 80 new candidate fits and 40 selected
evaluations using independent random namespaces and ten fresh seeds. Accordingly, the
final analytical/empirical report covers 500 candidate fits and 250 selected evaluations
across the original and repair experiments. The main study and the repair study are
separate evidence streams; their counts are not pooled into a fictitious single factorial
comparison.


5.4    Outcomes and statistical comparisons

The primary outcome is test misclassification probability. Accuracy and mean squared
error are secondary, while learned excess is the fitted test error minus the representation-
specific Bayes risk. Six planned origin contrasts compare DM − D0 and DA − D0 at
each of the three displacements. Paired seed differences are summarized by their means,
sample standard deviations and Student-t intervals with nine degrees of freedom.
Simultaneous 95% intervals use a Bonferroni adjustment across the six contrasts;
supplementary analyses retain whole-seed bootstrap sensitivity and pointwise intervals
for diagnostics.
   The analytical Bayes rule is also evaluated on the identical held-out observations,
allowing test-sampling variation to be distinguished from the numerical integration used
to calculate the population floor. Across the 15 main conditions, the mean empirical
Bayes-rule error differs from the integrated risk by at most 0.061 percentage points.
