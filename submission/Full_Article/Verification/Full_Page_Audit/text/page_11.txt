   Finite-origin positivity does not imply a globally positive lower bound. A sequence
of strictly positive values may converge to zero along increasingly distant centers. This
distinction is fundamental when the goal is to establish an irreducible error for every
possible normalization origin. Viewed from afar, rays through a bounded population
become almost parallel, so their retained differences are associated with projections
perpendicular to the viewing direction. The statistical statement needed here concerns
the optimal classification risks of those representations rather than a visual resemblance
between rays. The uniform theorem below controls all viewing directions simultaneously
and completes the origin-search problem at infinity.
