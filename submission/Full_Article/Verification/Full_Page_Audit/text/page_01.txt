      Global loss bounds and one-bit recovery for

                direction-normalized half-moons

                                Meshaal Mouawad
 Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Mississippi State University
                          Mississippi State, MS 39762, USA
                    Corresponding author: mm4922@msstate.edu



Abstract. Direction normalization retains the orientation of an observation from a
shared origin while discarding its distance. If that distance determines the optimal
class decision, a classifier trained on the normalized representation cannot recover
the lost distinction. We study this mechanism in a continuous half-moon family and
determine whether moving the normalization origin can prevent the resulting loss. An
exact ray-cancellation identity expresses additional Bayes error through class-preference
mass merged by direction. A uniform distant-origin limit to orthogonal-projection risk
and geometric criteria establish positive loss over every origin for specified disjoint
and overlapping half-moon configurations. Constructive certificates give explicit whole-
plane lower bounds. For one disjoint midpoint geometry, adding a common-threshold
near/far radius bit restores every class decision, with a sharp threshold interval and
measurement-error tolerance. Frozen neural studies contain 500 candidate fits and 250
selected evaluations. In the independent repair experiment, direction-only networks
made 54,362 errors among 500,000 observations; the one-bit networks made three. The
analysis separates representation loss from learning error and establishes a minimal
radial decision repair in the proved geometry.

Keywords: Direction normalization; Bayes risk; spatial sign; half-moon classification;
information preservation; radial quantization; one-bit decoder.
