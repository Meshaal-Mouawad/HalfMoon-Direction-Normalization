6     Neural results evaluated against population risk

6.1    Three geometries reveal different sources of error

Table 2 reports the population Bayes risk and mean learned test error for all 15
main geometry–representation conditions. The raw-coordinate risk gives the best
achievable decision from the untransformed observation. Each direction-only risk gives
the minimum error after deleting radius about its specified origin. The learned excess
is the difference between fitted-network error and the corresponding population floor.
This decomposition is necessary because two networks with different observed error
rates may simply have been trained on features with different attainable information.
    The negative-displacement case establishes that direction normalization can be
decision preserving for a suitable origin. At h = −0.25, raw coordinates R and
directions from the zero and midpoint origins all have zero Bayes risk and zero observed
test error in the main study. Directions from the upper origin DA , by contrast, have
population risk 22.2934% and mean neural test error 22.528%. The same underlying
classes and network training budget therefore produce drastically different attainable
risks solely because normalization uses a different origin. This is a negative control for
any overgeneralized claim that direction normalization always destroys class decisions.
    The disjoint h = 0.5 case isolates representation loss more sharply. Full coordinates
have zero population risk. Direction-only risks vary by origin: 11.8577% for D0 ,
10.7479% for the midpoint DM , and 21.1509% for DA . Their fitted networks exhibit
errors close to these respective floors. The midpoint network averages 10.835%, about
0.087 percentage points above the calculated midpoint Bayes risk. Most of its error
therefore follows from discarded radius rather than the inability of the trained network
