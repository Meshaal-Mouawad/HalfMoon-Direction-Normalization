within one seed are not treated as independent experimental replicates for confidence
intervals.


5.2     Feature construction and controlled training

The five input conditions in the main study are raw coordinates R; direction-only
features D0 , DM , DA ; and direction with full radius PA . Direction is calculated by
dividing the centered vector by its norm, with the explicitly specified zero-residual
convention. After transformation, every feature is standardized using only its training-
set mean and population standard deviation; a constant feature receives unit scale.
Because this transformation is invertible on each nonconstant coordinate, it does not
change the representation’s information content.
   The principal learner is a neural network with one hidden layer of 64 hyperbolic-
tangent units and a linear output. Training targets are labels −1 and +1, and the
fitted loss is mean squared error. Full-batch Adam [17] uses (β1 , β2 ) = (0.9, 0.999),
numerical ϵ = 10−8 , and learning rates 0.001 and 0.01. Each candidate is run for a
fixed budget of 4,000 optimizer updates. Validation is performed at initialization and
every 50 updates, and the selected checkpoint minimizes validation misclassification
error, breaking ties by validation mean squared error, earlier step and lower learning
rate.
   The hidden-layer width and training protocol are the same across the main condi-
tions; the number of parameters is not identical when feature dimension changes. A
two-input model has 257 parameters, and a three-input model has 321. The population-
risk comparisons isolate the loss from the representation itself. The empirical comparison
of fitted networks retains this parameter-count difference as part of its stated model
specification.
