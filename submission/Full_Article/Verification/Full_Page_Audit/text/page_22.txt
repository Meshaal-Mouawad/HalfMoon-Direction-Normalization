7     Independent empirical test of the one-bit repair

7.1    A new evaluation distinct from the original origin study

The one-bit theorem establishes an exact decoder on the continuous h = 0.5 supports.
The independent neural experiment asks whether a trained network can effectively
use the restricted feature. Ten new paired dataset/initialization seeds, 2001–2010,
generate area-uniform, equal-prior observations under a fresh random namespace. The
same sample sizes, fixed 64-unit architecture, two learning rates, 4,000-update budget,
feature conditioning and validation-based checkpoint selection are retained from the
original protocol. The bit cutoff is fixed at 0.8 before generating the repair evaluation
samples.
    Four representations clarify the comparison. Raw coordinates R and midpoint
direction plus continuous radius PM retain full positional information, establishing two
information-complete controls. Midpoint direction alone DM removes radius and has a
nonzero Bayes-risk floor. The repaired representation B contains midpoint direction
and the single near/far bit. The last two comparisons isolate the empirical value of
the bit relative to direction and the remaining gap relative to full radial information.
The 80 new candidate fits yield 40 selected models whose final tests are evaluated after
model selection is frozen.
    The study treats the paired B − DM and B − PM differences as their own two-
comparison family, with simultaneous 95% Bonferroni-adjusted intervals. The direction-
plus-bit and direction-plus-radius networks use three input features and 321 parameters;
the raw and direction-only controls use two features and 257 parameters. These details
matter because the evidence combines a change in the information supplied with a
documented change in input dimensionality.
