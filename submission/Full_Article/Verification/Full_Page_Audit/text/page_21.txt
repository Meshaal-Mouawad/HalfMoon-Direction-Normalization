   Five nonzero contrasts have simultaneous intervals excluding zero. In the disjoint
h = 0.5 case, the midpoint lowers fitted error by 1.156 percentage points relative to
the zero origin, while moving to the upper origin increases it by 9.379 points. In the
overlapping h = 1 case, both the midpoint and upper-origin transformations reduce
learned error relative to D0 , by 1.254 and 1.445 points, respectively. For h = −0.25,
the DM − D0 difference is zero for every seed. These results demonstrate that the
sign and magnitude of the origin effect are determined by class arrangement, not by a
universal rule that moving the origin in one direction helps.
   The population and neural findings are consistent in their ordering, yet they support
different scopes of inference. Paired neural comparisons establish differences under
the evaluated origins, model class and finite training protocol. The analytical all-
origin result establishes the absence of any lossless origin over the specified continuous
population. Neither is a substitute for the other: a proof over all origins does not
measure a network’s optimization success, and a finite experiment does not prove a
universal representation theorem.
