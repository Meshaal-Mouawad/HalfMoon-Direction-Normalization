6.3    Sensitivity to the sampling law and diagnostic networks

The second sampling law changes probabilities inside the same support geometry. Its
calculated risk values are preserved in the supplementary population table. For equal
priors, the ratio of radius-uniform to area-uniform class densities lies between 5/6 and
5/4, so the signed-risk argument gives a corresponding conservative relation between
normalization losses. In particular, changing density within the supports does not
remove the geometric obstruction that causes all-origin loss when both class densities
remain positive on their respective regions.
    The prespecified width and conditioning diagnostics delimit how the learned results
should be interpreted. At h = 1, the paired difference between the direction-plus-radius
control PA and raw coordinates R is 0.044 percentage points, with a pointwise 95%
interval of [−0.043, 0.130]. All three width-15 versus width-64 diagnostic intervals
include zero. Removing affine input conditioning increases DA error by 0.179 percentage
points, interval [0.053, 0.305]; the corresponding raw and full-radius diagnostic intervals
include zero. These are measured comparisons under specific model settings. Their
role is to characterize variation in the fitting procedure while the underlying Bayes-risk
interpretation remains fixed.
