answer different questions and are preserved separately. This separation of theorem,
decoder and learner prevents a high test accuracy from being interpreted as a universal

8.4    A reproducible sequence for testing class-preserving pre-

       processing

The combined analysis suggests a disciplined evaluation sequence for a direction-based
feature map. First establish the source-space Bayes error, because some class overlap is
intrinsic to the original observations. Then derive or estimate the Bayes error for the
transformed representation and attribute their difference to lost information. When a
normalization parameter such as the origin can vary, study its full admissible domain
rather than only a finite grid. Finally measure fitted-model error above the appropriate
transformed-input floor and test whether a restricted additional measurement can
preserve the needed decision.
   The frozen paired experiments instantiate this sequence. Their empirical outcomes
closely track the respective Bayes-risk floors across origins and distinguish those floors
from training excess. The independent repair study tests learnability of a theoretically
sufficient input rather than using neural accuracy to claim the existence of an exact
decoder. Together with the geometric proofs, these results demonstrate a specific
mechanism of decision-information loss and a precisely bounded method of recovery.
