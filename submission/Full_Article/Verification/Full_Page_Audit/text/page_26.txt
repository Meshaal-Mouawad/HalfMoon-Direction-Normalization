8.3      Decision recovery from a radial bit

The one-bit construction distinguishes data reconstruction from decision sufficiency.
Continuous radius paired with direction reconstructs the original coordinates, preserving
all information. The near/far bit cannot reconstruct a point and is uninformative
about its label in isolation under the symmetric prior model. Combined with direction,
however, it selects one of two angular rules, each correct on its prescribed radial region.
The repair therefore succeeds not by approximating the lost continuous radius, but by
preserving precisely the distinction required for the decision in this geometry.
   The threshold interval makes the constructive result useful beyond an isolated
nominal cutoff. Its endpoints are proved from the continuous supports, not estimated
from neural validation accuracy. The measured radius may have a bounded pertur-
bation without invalidating the rule, provided the cutoff and perturbation meet the
exact inequality. The experimental threshold and the maximum-tolerance threshold
answer different questions and are preserved separately. This separation of theorem,
decoder and learner prevents a high test accuracy from being interpreted as a universal
robustness guarantee.
