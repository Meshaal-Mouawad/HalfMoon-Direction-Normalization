   The disjoint h = 0.5 case isolates representation loss more sharply. Full coordinates
have zero population risk. Direction-only risks vary by origin: 11.8577% for D0 ,
10.7479% for the midpoint DM , and 21.1509% for DA . Their fitted networks exhibit
errors close to these respective floors. The midpoint network averages 10.835%, about
0.087 percentage points above the calculated midpoint Bayes risk. Most of its error
therefore follows from discarded radius rather than the inability of the trained network
to approximate the optimal directional rule. The underlying global theorem is stronger
than these finite comparisons: it states that no possible origin eliminates this type of
loss in the proved displacement range.
   In the overlapping h = 1 configuration, raw-coordinate ambiguity is already present
before preprocessing. The area-uniform raw-coordinate Bayes risk is approximately
6.7157%. The three directional features increase that floor to 21.7294%, 20.8548%,
and 20.5987%. A fitted raw-coordinate model achieves mean error 6.790%, and a
direction-plus-radius model achieves 6.833%, both near the common full-information
floor. The comparison distinguishes two errors that would otherwise be conflated: the
source-space overlap that even a coordinate-perfect classifier cannot eliminate, and the
additional ambiguity introduced by observing direction alone.


6.2    How much does changing the origin change learned error?

The six prespecified paired origin contrasts provide an inferential check on the pattern
seen in the population-risk table. Every seed contributes a paired difference computed on
the same held-out observations under two transformations. The simultaneous confidence
intervals therefore summarize across the ten independent seed-level repetitions rather
than treating hundreds of thousands of test observations as independent replicates for
the purpose of inference.
