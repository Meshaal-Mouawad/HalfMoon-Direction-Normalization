8     Discussion

8.1    The representation, rather than the learner, can determine

       the error floor

A common approach to preprocessing evaluation is to train a model under several feature
transformations and compare accuracies. The present study shows why that evidence
alone is insufficient for understanding direction normalization. When preprocessing
maps class-preference regions requiring different decisions to the same direction, the
transformed observation cannot carry the information needed for the raw-coordinate
Bayes rule. The resulting minimum error is imposed before model fitting. If learned
error is close to that minimum, more elaborate training may change the small excess
without recovering the missing decisions.
    The three half-moon arrangements clarify which conclusions are intrinsic to geometry.
At negative displacement, suitable origins remain decision preserving. At the disjoint
midpoint displacement, direction-only features have a positive floor for every origin even
though the original classes can be separated perfectly. At the overlapping displacement,
raw ambiguity and representational ambiguity coexist. These distinctions permit error
to be allocated to its correct cause instead of describing every increase in test error as
a network deficiency.
