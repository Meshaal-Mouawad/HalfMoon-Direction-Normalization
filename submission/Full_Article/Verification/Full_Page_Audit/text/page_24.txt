leaves the direction-only Bayes floor unchanged. The bit does not identify the label
independently of direction; under the equal-prior reflected sampling laws its own Bayes
error is 50%. Its value is relational: it supplies the small amount of radial information
needed to choose which angular separator applies.
    The sharp interval result adds a separate guarantee. With the same fixed decoder,
all common thresholds t in [α, β], where α ≈ 0.7433034374 and β ≈ 0.8120914734,
preserve every decision. If the measured radius differs from the true radius by at
most ε, exact recovery is guaranteed when α + ε ≤ t ≤ β − ε. At the experimental
cutoff t = 0.8, this yields a sharp guarantee of approximately 0.01209147 coordinate
units. The different threshold t⋆ ≈ 0.77769746 maximizes the guaranteed tolerance at
approximately 0.03439402 units. These are mathematical statements about a fixed
decoder and bounded measurement perturbation; the neural experiment uses the
specified exact-radius bit and does not test noisy-radius performance.



8     Discussion

8.1    The representation, rather than the learner, can determine

       the error floor

A common approach to preprocessing evaluation is to train a model under several feature
transformations and compare accuracies. The present study shows why that evidence
alone is insufficient for understanding direction normalization. When preprocessing
maps class-preference regions requiring different decisions to the same direction, the
transformed observation cannot carry the information needed for the raw-coordinate
Bayes rule. The resulting minimum error is imposed before model fitting. If learned
error is close to that minimum, more elaborate training may change the small excess
without recovering the missing decisions.
