5      Experimental methodology and reproducibility

5.1        Separating a representation limit from neural learning

The theoretical Bayes risk answers what the best classifier could do if the class probabil-
ities were known and the representation were fixed. A neural experiment answers how
close a finite-data learner comes to that risk. These are separate measurements: an error
forced by discarded radius remains even with unlimited model training, whereas error
above the Bayes floor may reflect insufficient training data, architecture approximation
or optimization. The empirical design therefore calculates or certifies the population
risk independently and compares every trained network with the floor appropriate to
its own inputs.
    To support paired comparisons, the same sampled observations are transformed into
each representation within a given geometry and seed. This reduces variation caused
merely by generating different test sets. The study uses ten final dataset/initialization
seeds, numbered 1001–1010, for the main experiment. For each seed it generates bal-
anced training, validation and held-out test sets of 2,000, 2,000 and 50,000 observations.
