9    Conclusion

Discarding radius during direction normalization can destroy a class distinction even
when the original class supports are disjoint. The study derives an exact population-risk
identity for that loss, establishes conditions under which it remains positive over every
normalization origin, and proves a uniform connection to projected classification at
distant origins. Explicit whole-plane certificates make the guarantee quantitative. In the
proved disjoint midpoint geometry, one shared near/far radius bit recovers every class
decision, with a sharp threshold interval and measurement-error margin. Controlled
neural evaluations show that trained models can approach the population limits
associated with raw coordinates, direction-only inputs and the repaired representation.
The contribution is a decision-based account of when an origin-dependent preprocessing
rule irreversibly removes information, and how a minimal radial code restores it in a
precisely characterized setting.
