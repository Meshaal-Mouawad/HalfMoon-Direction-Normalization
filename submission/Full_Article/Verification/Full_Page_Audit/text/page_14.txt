Recovering the decision with one radius bit. The all-origin loss result rules
out fixing the midpoint case by moving the origin, but it leaves open whether a small
amount of distance information can repair the representation. The proposed bit answers
only whether an observation is near or far from the fixed midpoint; it does not report
the full distance. At h = 0.5, direction alone has positive loss at every origin. At
c = (1/2, 1/4), append q = 1{∥x − c∥ > 4/5}. For q = 0, the sign of 9u1 + 10u2
determines the class; for q = 1, the sign of u2 − u1 determines it. Positive signs identify
class 0. Figure 2 shows how the radial bit assigns observations to the two angular
rules. Together, the two rules correctly label the complete class supports under their
respective bit conditions, giving zero Bayes risk under either sampling law and any
positive priors. Supplementary Section S11 proves the separating inequalities. Since
zero additional radial bits leave the positive direction-only floor, one bit is the minimal
radial addition at this geometry.
