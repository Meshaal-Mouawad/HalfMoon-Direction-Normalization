3.2    Sampling laws and what their comparison tests

The primary experimental distribution samples uniformly with respect to planar area
inside each class support, using equal class priors. This means that a region twice
as large has twice as much probability within a given class, independent of its radial
location. A separate distribution samples the local radius and semicircle angle uniformly;
after conversion to planar density, this gives greater density near the inner boundary
than the area-uniform law. The two models have identical supports and different
probability allocations. Their comparison tests whether the geometric obstruction
persists under changes in sampling measure, and how the numerical population risks
change.
   The frozen neural evaluations use only area-uniform samples. The radius-uniform
alternative contributes analytical population-risk results, not an additional neural
benchmark. Both are informative: the support geometry establishes qualitative decision-
loss conditions whenever class densities are strictly positive on the stated regions, while
the exact probability density determines the numerical amount of Bayes error. This
distinction is needed to interpret theorems about all origins alongside tables of calculated
risks for specified distributions.


3.3    Representation comparisons and their information content

The input representation is the independent variable of the neural evaluation. The
full-coordinate condition R retains the two Cartesian measurements. Three direction-
only conditions D0 , DM , DA normalize about the origin, the class-center midpoint, and
the upper reference point (0, 3). Their values differ because the shared center changes
which observations lie on the same ray. The direction-plus-radius condition PA retains
both quantities about the upper origin. Since the original point can be reconstructed
from those two quantities and the known center, it has the same population Bayes
risk as R, even though a network fitted to the two feature sets need not have identical
learned error.
