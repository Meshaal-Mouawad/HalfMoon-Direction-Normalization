Title
Exact radial coding and feedback limits for direction-normalized half-moons

Author
Meshaal Mouawad

Affiliation
Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Mississippi State University, 216 Simrall Hall, 406 Hardy Road, Mississippi State, MS 39762, USA

Corresponding author email

Abstract
Discarding distance can map two classes to the same direction. We determine the minimum communication needed to recover their labels when a classifier knows the direction and a separate sensor measures radius. A sufficient condition for an exact budget combines two geometric properties. A common list of cutoffs separates the classes along every ambiguous direction, and each successive radial interval conflicts with all preceding intervals over a set of directions of positive measure. Grouping the cutoffs attains the budget. The conflicts give a positive population error floor for every smaller budget, including for encoders with disconnected cells. For reflected half-annuli, a geometric recurrence gives the minimum one-way radial alphabet M, which grows logarithmically in the reciprocal of the normalized distance to contact. With a one-bit radial reply, exact classification before contact requires M−1 direction hints. A population that presents one pair of radial bands per direction needs only ⌈log₂ M⌉ hints for the same one-way minimum. At M = 13, the respective requirements are twelve and four hint values. At contact, countably many threshold choices suffice for the half-annuli, and every finite choice set incurs error.

Highlights
Positive-mass conflicts give lower bounds for every measurable radial encoder.
Cutoffs and ordered conflicts give exact tradeoffs between hint and reply sizes.
Precontact half-annuli with one-way minimum M need ⌈log₂ M⌉ total fixed-length bits.
With one reply bit, half-annuli need M−1 hints and pair-specific sectors ⌈log₂ M⌉.
Width ratio sets the coefficient of logarithmic radial alphabet growth near contact.

Novelty statement
The paper proves a sufficient condition for an exact communication tradeoff in continuous classification from a single observation. The condition combines cutoffs separating the classes along ambiguous rays with an ordered sequence of positive-mass radial conflicts. It yields matching constructions and lower bounds over all measurable radial encoders. For populations meeting the condition, if M radial symbols are needed without feedback, exact recovery with K hints and reply alphabet sizes m₁, …, mₖ is possible exactly when Σⱼ(mⱼ−1) ≥ M−1. Before contact the half-annuli satisfy the condition. With f feedback bits and b radial reply bits, feasibility is 2^f(2^b−1) ≥ M−1. The minimum total fixed-length budget for the half-annuli is ⌈log₂ M⌉ bits, and feedback can shift bits from the reply to the reverse link.
A matched comparison shows how the simultaneous conflicts govern communication. For a one-bit radial reply, half-annuli need M−1 hint values, and a population whose radial band pairs conflict on separate direction sectors needs ⌈log₂ M⌉. At displacement 0.77 the half-annuli need 13 one-way radial symbols. The two populations with that one-way minimum need 12 and 4 hint values, respectively, taking 4 and 2 fixed-length feedback bits. Optimizing the reply width as well gives minimum total costs of 4 bits for the half-annuli and 3 bits for the pair-specific population. At first contact, every fixed finite hint and reply budget leaves positive population error, and a countable threshold list attains exact recovery with one radial bit.
These results quantify decision-preserving compression of a continuous feature when the decoder has side information. Hanna et al. studied classification error on finite data with quantized features. Misra et al. studied quantizer selection by a side message for functional mean-squared error. Charpenay et al. established how answer vectors govern zero-error block coding with finite alphabets in their full-support case. The present proof uses positive-mass conflicts in continuous radius and direction to obtain exact budgets for a single observation.
